\begin{abstract}
Transparent fault tolerance---making an \emph{unmodified} server crash-recoverable
with no code changes---has repeatedly failed to reach production, not because
snapshotting is hard but because of three coupled costs that were all large at the
millisecond operating point every prior system lived at: logging non-determinism
(above all message order), coordinating a distributed consistent cut, and holding
externally-visible output until its producing state is durable. The industry instead
rewrote applications to externalize state---durable execution, exactly-once stream
processing---winning mindshare but leaving the legacy fleet behind. We revisit the
abandoned transparent path and show that the verdict is operating-point-dependent: it
becomes practical under two co-designed shifts, neither sufficient alone.

The first, and most-measured, shift is a user-space \emph{determinism libOS}
(\texttt{LD\_PRELOAD}, no kernel or application changes) that makes unmodified servers
deterministically recoverable by virtualizing their residual local
non-determinism---time, randomness, and thread scheduling---at a few-percent
steady-state overhead; we demonstrate \emph{byte-identical deterministic recovery} of
stock Redis, Memcached, Nginx, and Node.js, and general CRIU checkpoint/restore of a
multi-process PostgreSQL. The second shift is the operating point: routing those
servers through a microsecond-scale in-network total-order reliable fabric (1Pipe)
collapses all three coupled costs. The fabric \emph{is} the order, so the replay
order-log vanishes; a timestamp-$T$ snapshot replaces the Chandy--Lamport cut; and the
output-commit barrier \emph{coincides} with the fabric's reliable-delivery commit
barrier, so the recovery log---replicated by scattering each input to the replicas in
one RTT (1Pipe's native replication)---is durable within a barrier the system already
pays for, so fault tolerance adds no round trip over the reliable-fabric baseline---a
margin that is zero by construction, not a small measured number. We quantify the overlap ($0.23\%$ marginal cost in the loopback reproduction;
corroborated by real RDMA verbs at $\sim$1.5\,\textmu s over SoftRoCE), verify
exactly-once and linearizability under injected crashes, machine-check the core
protocols in TLA+, and position the result honestly against LLFT and HyCoR, which
substantially anticipate the idea at the millisecond scale. The contribution is a
realization-and-measurement: at the in-network-total-order, microsecond operating
point, a long-dismissed technique becomes a byproduct of the communication fabric.
\end{abstract}
