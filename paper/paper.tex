\begin{abstract}
Transparent fault tolerance makes an \emph{unmodified} server crash-recoverable with no code
changes. It has never reached production, not because snapshots are hard but because of three
coupled costs that are large at the millisecond operating point of every prior system: logging
non-determinism (above all message order), coordinating a consistent distributed cut, and holding
externally-visible output until its producing state is durable. Industry instead rewrote
applications to externalize state, leaving the legacy fleet behind. We revisit the transparent path
and show the verdict is operating-point-dependent: it becomes practical under two co-designed shifts.

The first is a user-space \emph{determinism libOS} (\texttt{LD\_PRELOAD}, no kernel or
application changes) that makes unmodified servers deterministically recoverable by
virtualizing their residual local non-determinism, namely time, randomness, and thread
scheduling, at a few-percent overhead. We demonstrate \emph{byte-identical recovery} of
stock Redis, Memcached, Nginx, and Node.js, and CRIU checkpoint/restore of a multi-process
PostgreSQL.

The second is the operating point: routing those servers through a microsecond in-network
total-order reliable fabric (1Pipe) collapses all three costs. The fabric \emph{is} the
order, so the replay order-log vanishes; a timestamp-$T$ snapshot replaces the
Chandy--Lamport cut; and, the crux, the output-commit barrier and the fabric's
reliable-delivery commit barrier are the \emph{same} barrier, so fault tolerance adds no
round trip over the reliable-fabric baseline. We quantify the overlap ($0.23\%$ marginal
cost), verify exactly-once and linearizability under injected crashes, and machine-check
the protocols in TLA+. The contribution is a realization-and-measurement, positioned
honestly against LLFT and HyCoR, which anticipate the idea at the millisecond scale: at
the in-network-total-order, microsecond operating point, a long-dismissed technique
becomes a byproduct of the communication fabric.
\end{abstract}
